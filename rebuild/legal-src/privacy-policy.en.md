## 2. Summary

- We collect only what we need to run Zyflow: your account, the routes and content you create, and technical data to keep the app working.
- Your routes are private unless **you decide to share a specific route** with the community.
- We do not sell your data and we do not show ads in the app.
- Product analytics use a pseudonymous user ID, not your email address, and only run if you agree.
- Location is used only while the app is in use. There is no background location tracking.
- Push notifications reach you only if you allow them on your phone. News and offers, by push or by email, stay off until you turn them on.
- You can delete your account in the app at any time.

## 3. What data we process, why, and on what legal basis

### 3.1 Account data

**Data:** email address, full name, optional phone number, user ID, sign-in method, account creation date. If you sign in with Apple or Google, we receive your email address and, if you allow it, your name from that provider (with Apple you can hide your real email address). With Google sign-in we also receive a link to your Google profile picture, which we store with your profile and show only to you.

**Purpose:** to create and manage your account, sign you in and provide the service.

**Legal basis:** performance of a contract (Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR).

**Profile photo (optional):** if you add a profile photo, we store it in private file storage; it is visible only to you. You can change or remove it at any time in your profile, and it is deleted when you delete your account.

### 3.2 Location data

**Data:** precise location of your device, **only while the app is in use** and only if you grant the location permission. We do not collect location in the background.

**Purpose:** show your position on the map, plan and navigate routes, find nearby places and show weather and traffic alerts.

**Legal basis:** performance of a contract (Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR). Access to location on your device is controlled by your operating system permission, which you can withdraw at any time in your device settings. Without it, some features will not work.

Your live position is not stored on our servers. It is stored only if it becomes part of something you save, share or submit (see 3.3 to 3.5). When you collect a stamp, your position is used to check that you are at the place and is not stored; the stamp records the place and the time (see 3.17). Some location data is sent to map, search and routing services to answer your request (see 3.11).

### 3.3 Routes (saved, planned and imported)

**Data:** routes you plan and save, routes you import from GPX, FIT or TCX files or from a connected service (3.15), route names, notes, distance, duration, elevation and other route details, and saved places.

**Purpose:** to let you plan, save, export and reuse routes.

**Legal basis:** performance of a contract (Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR).

**Visibility:** saved routes are **private** and visible only to you, unless you share a route with the community (3.4).

Please note that a saved route is a record of where you have been or plan to go.

### 3.4 Community route sharing (optional, per route)

You can choose to share an individual saved route with the Zyflow community. Nothing is shared automatically; you decide for each route.

**What becomes visible when you share a route:** the full route GPS track (all points, including the start and end point), route name, your optional description, region, difficulty, distance, duration, elevation and surface details, an automatically generated map image, and any photos you add.

**Who can see it:** all signed-in Zyflow users. Route photos and map images are stored in publicly accessible storage, which means **anyone who has the link to a photo or image can open it**, also without a Zyflow account. Your name and email address are not shown with the shared route, but the route is linked to your user ID in our database.

> **Important:** the start and end points of a route can reveal where you live, work or regularly park your bike. Before sharing, consider starting and ending the route a little away from your home, and do not include personal details or photos that identify people, house numbers or licence plates.

**Removing a shared route:** you can remove a shared route at any time. This removes it from the community, so other users can no longer see it, and deletes its photos from our file storage. If you also want its map images removed from our file storage, write to [legal@zyflow.eu](mailto:legal@zyflow.eu) and we will remove them within 30 days; they are also deleted automatically when you delete your account. Copies that other users have already saved to their own accounts may remain in their accounts.

**Purpose:** to let riders discover and reuse routes shared by others.

**Legal basis:** performance of a contract at your request (Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR) – sharing is a feature you actively choose to use for each route.

### 3.5 Place reports and missing-place submissions

**Data:** when you confirm or report whether a point of interest (for example a charger, repair station or water tap) exists or works, we store your report, any notes, the time and your user ID. When you report a missing or wrong place, we store the location, the details you enter, your user ID, your email address and the time.

**Visibility:** place reports are shown to other users as community signals without your name. Missing-place submissions are seen only by our team during review. Approved places become part of the Zyflow places database without any link to you.

**Purpose:** to keep place information accurate for all riders, and to inform you about the status of your submission by email.

**Legal basis:** legitimate interest in an accurate, community-verified places database (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR). Please do not include personal data in notes.

### 3.6 Bike passport (optional)

**Data:** bike make, model, colour, frame number, purchase date, purchase value, notes and bike photos, and optionally your rider weight.

**Purpose:** to let you keep a record of your bike (for example to help in case of theft or for insurance) and to generate a bike passport document on your device. **Rider weight is optional and is used only for route calculations** (for example effort and time estimates). It is not used for any other purpose and is never shared.

**Visibility:** private to you. Bike photos are stored in private storage.

**Legal basis:** performance of a contract (Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR). All fields are voluntary and you can delete them at any time.

### 3.7 Camera and photo library

If you allow it, the app uses the camera and photo library only when you choose to add a photo (bike photos, route photos, place reports, profile photo) or save an image. We do not access your photos in the background. You can withdraw the permission in your device settings.

### 3.8 Emails

We use Brevo to store your contact details (email address, full name and user ID) and to send emails from Zyflow. Creating an account adds your contact details to Brevo, but **not to any mailing list**.

**Service emails:** account and security emails (for example sign-in codes and confirmations when you change your email address) and updates about places you submitted. **Legal basis:** performance of a contract (Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR).

**Marketing emails ("Ride ideas and Zyflow news"):** we send these **only if you turn them on yourself** in the app under Profile → Settings → Notifications → By email. The option is never switched on for you. When you turn it on, we add you to our mailing list at Brevo; when you turn it off, we remove you from it. We record your choice, when you made it and where in the app, so that we can show what you agreed to. You can withdraw consent at any time in the app, via the unsubscribe link in each email or by writing to [legal@zyflow.eu](mailto:legal@zyflow.eu). **Legal basis:** consent (Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR and ZEKom-2).

**What we send today:** marketing emails go only to people who turned them on. When you turn them on, you receive a short series of welcome emails about Zyflow, followed by occasional news. If you don't turn them on, you receive no marketing emails, including welcome emails.

### 3.9 Product analytics

We use PostHog (EU cloud) to understand how the app is used and to improve it. We send a pseudonymous **user ID (not your email address or name)**, selected in-app events (for example "route planned" or "GPX exported"), app version and basic device information. If you agree, PostHog also records technical details of app errors (the error message and where in the app it happened) so we can fix them. Automatic capture of all interactions and session recording are not used in the app. PostHog is set to **discard your IP address**.

**Legal basis:** your consent (Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR and ZEKom-2). The app asks you before analytics start; analytics are off until you agree. You can withdraw consent at any time in Settings → Share usage statistics; this does not affect processing before withdrawal.

### 3.10 Crash and performance monitoring

We use Sentry (EU data region) to detect and fix errors. Error and performance reports contain your user ID, technical details of the error, device model, operating system and app version, and your **IP address**, which is stored with error reports. Sentry's data scrubbing is enabled to remove sensitive values such as email addresses.

**Legal basis:** legitimate interest in a stable and secure app (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR).

### 3.11 Maps, search, routing and weather

To answer your requests, some data is sent to the following services. Each of them also sees an IP address as part of normal internet traffic.

- **Map tiles (MapTiler):** your device requests map tiles directly, so MapTiler sees your IP address and the map area you view.
- **Place search (Photon by komoot):** your search text is sent through our own server, so Photon does not see your IP address.
- **Reverse geocoding (Nominatim, OpenStreetMap Foundation):** your device sends coordinates directly to turn them into an address, so Nominatim sees your IP address and those coordinates.
- **Routing (our own OSRM server at [osrm.zyflow.eu](http://osrm.zyflow.eu) and GraphHopper):** route coordinates are sent to our self-hosted routing server (hosted on Contabo servers in Germany, EU) and, through our backend, to GraphHopper.
- **Fallback routing (public OSRM demo server):** if our own routing server is unavailable, the app may use the public OSRM demo server (router.project-osrm.org, provided by the OSRM project, project-osrm.org, which is based on OpenStreetMap) to calculate your route. In that case your device sends the route coordinates directly to that server, which therefore also sees your IP address. We do not control this server.
- **Weather (Open-Meteo):** an approximate location is sent through our backend.
- **Translation (DeepL):** we translate public traffic-alert texts. No personal data about you is sent.

**Legal basis:** performance of a contract (Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR).

### 3.12 Accommodation links (Stay22)

Some places show accommodation links from our affiliate partner Stay22. **Only if you tap such a link**, the link opens Stay22's service and passes the coordinates and name of that place (not your account data). Links to Stay22 may be affiliate links: if you book through them, we may receive a commission. After you open the link, Stay22 and the booking sites it uses are independent controllers and their own privacy policies apply.

**Legal basis:** legitimate interest in financing the free app (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR); the transfer happens only on your action.

### 3.13 Security and abuse prevention

Our search function stores the IP address of the requesting device together with request counters for a short time to prevent abuse (rate limiting). We keep these records only as long as needed to prevent abuse and delete them regularly. Search texts are cached for up to 24 hours without any link to you or your IP address. Our hosting providers keep technical server logs (including IP addresses) for security.

**Legal basis:** legitimate interest in the security of our service (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR).

### 3.14 Data stored on your device

The app stores your login session, your last known location, your recent searches, the date it last offered you notifications, stamps waiting to be sent while you have no signal (with the GPS reading taken at the place and any answers) and the places it reminded you about today locally on your device so that the app works quickly, you stay signed in, stamps collected without signal are not lost and we do not ask about notifications or remind you too often. This storage is strictly necessary for the service you request. It is removed when you uninstall the app.

If you agree to product analytics, PostHog also stores a pseudonymous identifier on your device; without your consent it stores nothing. Sentry stores a pseudonymous identifier for crash reporting (see 3.10).

### 3.15 Connected services (optional)

You can connect your Zyflow account to other cycling services. Connecting is always optional, you choose each service yourself, and you can disconnect at any time in Profile → Connected services.

| Service | What you can do | What we receive and store |
|---|---|---|
| **Strava** (Strava, Inc., USA) | Import your rides and your saved Strava routes | Your Strava athlete ID; the rides and routes you choose to import (name, GPS track, distance, elevation, date) |
| **Garmin Connect** (Garmin Ltd. and its affiliates) | Send routes to your Garmin device; import finished rides | Your Garmin user ID and the permissions you granted; routes you send; rides you choose to import |
| **Wahoo** (Wahoo Fitness L.L.C., USA) | Send routes to your Wahoo bike computer; import finished rides | Your Wahoo user ID; routes you send; rides you choose to import |

**How it works:** when you connect a service, you sign in on that service's own page; Zyflow never sees your password there. The service gives us an access token, which we store **encrypted** and use only to do what you ask: send a route you chose, list your rides or routes so you can pick what to import, and import the items you select. When you send a route, we keep a note of where it went, so we can show "Sent to …" and remove the copy if you delete the route in Zyflow.

**Visibility:** data from connected services is shown **only to you**. It is never published to the community, and routes imported from Strava cannot be shared. We never sell this data or use it to train artificial intelligence models.

**Disconnecting:** when you disconnect a service, or delete your Zyflow account, we revoke our access at that service and delete the stored token and user ID. When you disconnect **Strava**, we also delete the rides and routes you imported from Strava. Routes imported from other services stay in your saved routes until you delete them. Copies of routes we sent to a device service remain in your account there; delete them in that service's app if you no longer want them.

**Legal basis:** performance of a contract at your request (Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR). Each service is an independent controller for the data it holds about you; its own privacy policy applies.

### 3.16 Notifications (push and in the app)

**When we ask:** the app does not ask about notifications when you open it or when you sign up. After you save a route or a place, it may offer to let you know about it, for example when the weather is good for that route. You can also turn notifications on yourself in the app's notification inbox. Your phone's own permission prompt appears only after you tap "Turn on notifications". If you tap "Not now", the app does not offer again for at least 14 days. You can allow or block notifications for Zyflow at any time in your phone's settings.

**What we store:** if you allow notifications, the app gets a push token for your phone through our push provider Expo. A push token is an address for the Zyflow app on that phone; it contains no name, email address or location. We store the push token with your user ID, the date it was created and last updated, and the build number of your phone's operating system. We also store your notification settings and the notifications we send you, which you can read in the app's notification inbox: title, text, type, time, whether it was sent by push and when you read it.

**How push notifications are delivered:** we pass each push notification to Expo (650 Industries, Inc., USA), which hands it to Apple Push Notification service (iPhone) or Google's Firebase Cloud Messaging (Android) to deliver it to your phone; on Android we can also send it to Firebase Cloud Messaging directly. They receive the push token, the title and text of the notification and an internal notification ID. The text can name a route or place you saved. We do not send your email address or your location with a push notification. Apple and Google deliver push notifications under their own terms and privacy policies.

**What decides what we send:** the routes and places you saved, your notification settings and public data such as weather forecasts. We do not use your live location for notifications and do not track your location in the background.

**Notification groups and defaults:**

| Group | Examples | Default after you allow notifications | Legal basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Your rides & places** | Severe weather, battery and charging alerts for your rides and saved places | On | Contract, Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR |
| **Your contributions** | Updates on places you suggested, approved partner access | On | Contract, Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR |
| **News & offers** | Zyflow announcements, new offers and promotions | Off until you turn it on | Consent, Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR and ZEKom-2 |

The first two groups are part of the service you use. News & offers are sent only if you turn them on; you can turn them off again at any time, without affecting what was sent before.

**Changing your choice:** under Profile → Settings → Notifications you can turn each group of notifications on or off, both as push notifications and in the inbox. You can also block all Zyflow notifications in your phone's settings; then no push notifications reach you, but the groups you left on still appear in the app's inbox.

### 3.17 Stamp book

**What it is:** when you are at a place (within about 150 metres), you can collect a stamp for it in the app. A stamp is collected only when you stamp it yourself; nothing is stamped automatically.

**Data:** when you collect a stamp, the app sends your current position, its GPS accuracy and the time of the reading to our server. The server checks that you are at the place, that the reading is recent and that you could have travelled there since your previous stamp. Your position is used only for this check and is **not stored**. We store the stamp: the place, its level (bronze or silver), the date and time of each counted visit (at most one per place per day), how far from the place you were, the GPS accuracy and your user ID. If you answer questions about the place for a silver stamp, your answers are stored as place reports (3.5).

**Reminders in the app:** while the Home screen is open and you have allowed location, the app can remind you when you have been near a place you have not stamped yet for about two minutes. This happens only while you use the app. To find places near you, the app asks our server for places around your position; the position is not stored, and nothing about the reminder is stored on our servers.

**Without signal:** if you have no signal, the stamp, the GPS reading taken at the place and any answers wait on your phone and are sent when you are back online; the server then makes the same checks. Once sent, they are removed from your phone.

**Visibility:** your stamp book is **private** and visible only to you. Other users see only counts without names, for example how many riders have a stamp at a place or have finished a collection.

**Purpose:** to let you collect stamps for the places you visit by bike, and to keep stamps fair.

**Legal basis:** performance of a contract (Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR); collecting stamps is a feature you choose to use.

Please note that your stamps are a record of the places you have visited and when.

## 8. Your rights

Under the GDPR you have the right to:

- **access** your personal data and receive a copy (Art. 15);
- **rectification** of inaccurate or incomplete data (Art. 16) – most data you can correct yourself in the app;
- **erasure** ("right to be forgotten", Art. 17);
- **restriction** of processing (Art. 18);
- **data portability** – receive data you provided in a structured, machine-readable format, or have it sent to another controller (Art. 20); routes can also be exported as GPX in the app;
- **object** to processing based on legitimate interest, for example crash monitoring (Art. 21), and to object at any time to direct marketing;
- **withdraw consent** at any time, without affecting the lawfulness of processing before the withdrawal (Art. 7(3));
