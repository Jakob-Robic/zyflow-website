"Bike-friendly" gets used a lot. A sticker on a door, a line on a booking site, a rack that fits two bikes on a good day. For riders, it should mean something more useful: you can arrive by bike, leave it somewhere you trust, and enjoy the place without worrying about what's happening outside.

## what we look for

In Zyflow, a place counts as bike-friendly when it offers real facilities for riders, not just goodwill. The ones that matter most:

- **Bike parking.** Somewhere proper to lock up, close to the entrance
- **Secure storage.** A locked room, garage or shed, especially important for stays and expensive e-bikes
- **A pump and basic tools.** Small things that save a ride
- **Charging.** A socket for an e-bike, ideally somewhere you can sit and wait

## where the information comes from

We combine open map data, regional tourism data from partners like Open Data Hub, and details that businesses add themselves. When a hotel, café or restaurant manages its own listing, the information tends to be more accurate and more current. That's why we're making it free for places to list and update themselves while we launch.

> You can arrive by bike, leave it somewhere you trust, and enjoy the place without worrying about what's happening outside.

## how to find these places in the app

Open Explore and switch on the layers you care about: Stay, Coffee or Restaurants. Then turn on Bike-friendly only. The map now shows just the places with real facilities for riders, so you can plan a lunch stop or a night's stay knowing the bike is taken care of too.

For peace of mind, you can also turn on the Safety layer to see bike theft by area. It's a simple way to decide where secure storage really matters.

## run a place riders love?

If you have a rack, a storage room or a spare socket, riders want to know. Request access to the partner portal, add your facilities and photos, and your place shows up for riders who filter for exactly that.
